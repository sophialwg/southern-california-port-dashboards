Dashboard and Purpose

Every container that moves through the Ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach eventually crosses a marine terminal gate on a truck. This dashboard tracks that activity by detailing daily, monthly, and yearly gate moves from 2019 to 2025, identifying the busiest days of the week, and showing how the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted and reshaped the pattern. Together, these views build a picture of when and how intensely the ports' surrounding communities experience truck traffic.

This dashboard focuses on truck gate-move volume. Its companion, the Air Quality Dashboard, focuses on air-quality monitoring data, including, PM2.5 and black carbon readings near both ports. Viewed together, the two help build a fuller picture of the conditions experienced by nearby communities, without assuming a conclusion the data alone can't support. They should not be interpreted as direct measurements of air pollution or individual health risk.

Looking for air-quality data? PM2.5 and black-carbon monitoring trends, exceedance timelines, and hour-of-day pollution patterns for both ports live in the separate Air Quality Dashboard, which serves as the dashboard's companion piece.

 

Understanding the Data

This dashboard measures truck gate-move volume. It does not, on its own, measure air quality, emissions, or health outcomes. For that data, please see the companion Air Quality Dashboard.

Key Concepts

Everything on this dashboard builds on these four ideas.

  1. 1

    Gate Move

    One truck entering or exiting a marine terminal to pick up or drop off a container. This is the basic unit of activity tracked throughout this dashboard.

  2. 2

    7-Day Rolling Average

    Each day averaged with the six before it, smoothing out weekend closures to reveal the underlying trend beneath the daily ups and downs.

  3. 3

    Multi-Year Comparison

    How the same calendar month compares across 2019 through 2025, at both ports.

  4. 4

    COVID-19 Baseline

    2019 activity used as the pre-pandemic reference point for measuring how far volumes fell, and how fully they later recovered.

Multi-Year Gate Move Comparison

How does monthly gate-move activity compare across seven years and how did POLB's pandemic-era pattern differ from POLA's?

Monthly gate move totals across 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025.

Read the full chart guide

This chart plots the total gate moves for each month across seven years as seven separate lines on the same graph. Each point represents how many truck trips occurred at the Port of Long Beach during that calendar month in that year. Unlike the Port of LA, the Port of Long Beach saw activity increase through 2020 and peak in 2021, reflecting a different operational response to the pandemic and the surge in consumer-goods imports that followed.

  • Each colored line represents one year. Points on the line are monthly totals. Higher points mean more truck activity that month.
  • Where lines converge, activity levels were similar across years for that month.
  • Where lines diverge, something unusual happened — a pandemic, a shipping surge, or seasonal variation — that changed how many trucks moved through the port.
  • Use the toggle buttons to show or hide individual years for a cleaner comparison.
  • Hover over any point to see the exact monthly total for each year.

Key finding: Unlike the Port of LA, where 2020 was the lowest year, the Port of Long Beach saw its annual total increase in 2020 compared with 2019, rising from 3,972,750 to 4,108,658 — a 3.4% increase. This is consistent with broader Port of Long Beach reporting showing container volume rose 6.3% in 2020 compared with 2019, despite a decline in cargo during the first half of the year.
